Warframe

Digital Extremes|Digital Extremes|2013|Third Person · Action · RPG · Co-op · MMORPG
8.6106,930 reviews
PCPS5PS4Xbox X|SXbox OneSwitchiOS

Warframe pairs third-person action systems with hack & slash and sci-fi, and it is a stronger game for it. Progression is generous but earned, with new tools arriving exactly when the challenge curve demands them. From Digital Extremes, Warframe is a third-person action game where hack & slash and sci-fi drives almost every decision. The pacing alternates between tense build-up and explosive payoff, which keeps long sessions from flattening out. Controls are readable within minutes and deep enough that advanced players keep finding new tech. Post-launch support has been steady, with content drops that add rather than dilute. A full single-player campaign is included and can be finished without ever touching the online modes. It is free to play, with cosmetics-first monetisation rather than pay-to-win shortcuts.

Highlights

  • Photo mode with pose, lens and lighting controls
  • Camera tuned for both close-range melee and long-range shooting
  • Cover-driven combat with contextual vaults, slides and dodges
  • Escalating set pieces that always hand control back to the player
  • Photo mode with depth-of-field, focal length and filter controls
  • Weighty melee and firearm combat with animation-driven hit reactions
  • Combo chains with style-ranking feedback
  • Speculative technology with in-world justification

How it plays

  1. 01Objective — Hold angles, manage ammunition and convert map control into an objective rather than a body count.
  2. 02Play the objective. Kills create space, but objective progress wins matches.
  3. 03Rebind grenade, melee and ping to inputs you can reach without releasing movement.
  4. 04Learn two maps by concentrating on one objective route rather than roaming the whole layout.
  5. 05Complete the opening training range so sensitivity and field of view are locked in before matchmaking.
  6. 06Pick one weapon class and stay with it for your first ten matches so recoil becomes muscle memory.
  7. 07Use audio cues — footsteps, reloads and ability calls — to pre-aim before contact.
