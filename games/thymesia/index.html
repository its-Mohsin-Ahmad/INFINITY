Thymesia

OverBorder Studio|Team17|2022|Action · RPG
7.456,082 reviews
PCPS5Xbox X|SSwitch

Thymesia pairs action systems with soulslike and monster hunt, and it is a stronger game for it. Progression is generous but earned, with new tools arriving exactly when the challenge curve demands them. Thymesia marks another action entry from OverBorder Studio, and soulslike and monster hunt is the thread that ties it together. Post-launch support has been steady, with content drops that add rather than dilute. Systems interlock cleanly, so mastery in one area visibly improves outcomes in another. Every mechanic feeds the fantasy: if it looks like it should work, the simulation generally allows it. A full single-player campaign is included and can be finished without ever touching the online modes. It is a premium release with no live-service grind bolted on.

Highlights

  • Weighty melee and firearm combat with animation-driven hit reactions
  • Photo mode with depth-of-field, focal length and filter controls
  • Adaptive difficulty that scales aggression, not enemy health
  • Crafting, enchanting and upgrade loops tied to exploration
  • Endgame progression with ascending difficulty tiers
  • Dialogue choices that persist long after the conversation ends
  • Lost-currency retrieval and shortcut-driven level design
  • Creature design grounded in practical-effect horror

single playercampaigncross platform

How it plays

  1. 01Objective — Chain offence and defence mid-combat, reading enemy tells before committing to a heavy attack.
  2. 02Upgrade one weapon fully before buying a second; scaling beats variety early on.
  3. 03Start on standard difficulty and use early encounters to learn the dodge window rather than trading hits.
  4. 04Practise cancelling heavy attacks into a dodge until the timing feels automatic.
  5. 05Keep a ranged or throwable option for enemies that punish pure melee aggression.
  6. 06Set aside time for optional challenges: they usually unlock the strongest tools in the game.
  7. 07Use the environment — ledges, hazards and choke points — to control multi-enemy fights.
