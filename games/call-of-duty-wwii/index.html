Call of Duty: WWII

Sledgehammer Games|Activision|2017|FPS · Action · Multiplayer · Story Rich
7.541,644 reviews
PCPS4Xbox One

Call of Duty: WWII is the rare first-person shooter release that treats military and historical as a core mechanic rather than a bullet point. The presentation is confident — strong art direction, an adaptive score and clean, uncluttered HUD work. Sledgehammer Games built Call of Duty: WWII as a first-person shooter sandbox first and a showcase second, with military and historical as the spine. Controls are readable within minutes and deep enough that advanced players keep finding new tech. Difficulty is a dial rather than a wall — assists can be layered without ever feeling like training wheels. Accessibility is treated as a first-class feature, covering remapping, subtitles and timing tolerance. A full single-player campaign is included and can be finished without ever touching the online modes. It is a premium release with no live-service grind bolted on.

Highlights

  • Sensitivity, FOV and controller response fully configurable
  • Gunplay tuned for controller and mouse alike, with no aim-assist tax
  • Destruction and bullet penetration modelled on real materials
  • Weighty melee and firearm combat with animation-driven hit reactions
  • Escalating set pieces that always hand control back to the player
  • Photo mode with depth-of-field, focal length and filter controls
  • Historically grounded settings, equipment and tactics
  • Ranked seasons with visible rating changes and placement matches

How it plays

  1. 01Objective — Hold angles, manage ammunition and convert map control into an objective rather than a body count.
  2. 02Pick one weapon class and stay with it for your first ten matches so recoil becomes muscle memory.
  3. 03Practise hip-fire versus aim-down-sights at three distances: close, mid and long.
  4. 04Learn two maps by concentrating on one objective route rather than roaming the whole layout.
  5. 05Rebind grenade, melee and ping to inputs you can reach without releasing movement.
  6. 06Use audio cues — footsteps, reloads and ability calls — to pre-aim before contact.
  7. 07Complete the opening training range so sensitivity and field of view are locked in before matchmaking.
