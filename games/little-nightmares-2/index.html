Little Nightmares II

Tarsier Studios|Bandai Namco Entertainment|2021|Horror · Adventure · Puzzle
8.494,269 reviews
PCPS5PS4Xbox X|SXbox OneSwitch

Tarsier Studios returns with Little Nightmares II, a horror title that leans hard into platformer and psychological horror. Session length is flexible, so a run can be fifteen minutes or a full evening without either feeling wrong. Little Nightmares II marks another horror entry from Tarsier Studios, and platformer and psychological horror is the thread that ties it together. Post-launch support has been steady, with content drops that add rather than dilute. Systems interlock cleanly, so mastery in one area visibly improves outcomes in another. Performance scales well across hardware, with sensible presets and no pointless bloat in the settings menu. A full single-player campaign is included and can be finished without ever touching the online modes. It is a premium release with no live-service grind bolted on.

Highlights

  • Multiple endings driven by in-run choices
  • Optional one-life mode for the committed
  • Sound design that prioritises spatial cues over cheap jumps
  • Accessibility suite covering navigation, timing and colour
  • Companion and dialogue systems with branching responses
  • Environment-driven storytelling with no cut-scene interruptions
  • Collectibles and time trials per stage
  • No-combat stretches that rely on atmosphere

Modes

single playercampaigncross platform

How it plays

  1. 01Objective — Manage light, noise and inventory so that every encounter is one you chose to pick.
  2. 02Note where threats patrol; routes that were dangerous early often become quiet later.
  3. 03Keep your inventory light enough to pick up anything you find.
  4. 04Learn the safe room locations and treat them as planning points, not just save points.
  5. 05Check every room for resources, but never linger once you have what you need.
  6. 06Set brightness deliberately — too dark hides mechanics, too bright kills the atmosphere.
  7. 07Take breaks. Tension degrades decision-making faster than it degrades the game.
